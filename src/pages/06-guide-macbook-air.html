Can a MacBook Air play PC games?

Yes. In 572 player reports from Macs with the chips MacBook Airs use, M1 to M5, 85% rate a Windows game playable or perfect through Wine. The Air has no fan, so demanding games slow down in long sessions.

From players' reports in Highball's compatibility database; not tested with Portnaut on a MacBook Air · Updated 10 November 2026

What limits a MacBook Air

  • No fan. The Air cools itself passively, so in a long session with a demanding game it lowers its speed to stay cool. Lighter games don't get there.
  • Memory. With 8 GB, stay with lighter and older games. Large open-world games can use most of 16 GB on their own.
  • Translation costs speed. Rosetta translates the game's Intel code and a renderer translates its DirectX graphics to Metal, so expect less than a PC with similar hardware.
  • Anti-cheat. Games with kernel-level anti-cheat don't run through Wine on any Mac, and some games run only offline.

How to play PC games on a MacBook Air

  1. Check for a Mac version first. On Steam, look for the Apple logo on the game's page.
  2. Download Portnaut, open it, and choose Prepare Portnaut.
  3. Create a bottle for your game store and keep easy mode on, so Portnaut picks the renderer for each game.
  4. Install the game and press Play. Start at a lower resolution and raise settings while it stays smooth.

Questions

Is a MacBook Air good for gaming?

For lighter, older and well-optimised games, yes. For recent demanding games it works at lower settings, and it slows down in long sessions because it has no fan. A MacBook Pro holds its speed longer.

Can a MacBook Air run Steam games?

Yes. Games with a Mac version run in Steam's Mac app. Windows-only Steam games run through Wine: players report many working on the Air's chips.

Can an M1 MacBook Air play PC games?

Yes. The plain M1 has more reports than any other chip in the database. As one example, players measured GTA V's benchmark at about 50 to 60 fps at 1080p on a base M1.

Do I need Windows on my MacBook Air?

No. Wine provides the parts of Windows that games use, so there's no copy of Windows and no licence to buy. Boot Camp doesn't exist on Apple silicon.
